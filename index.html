Michele Banfi

PhD Researcher in Quantum Error Correction · Politecnico di Milano

I am a PhD student in Information Technology at Politecnico di Milano. My research is in quantum computing, with a focus on quantum error correction (QEC) and quantum compiler architectures:

  • QLDPC & Stabilizer Codes: designing high-rate quantum low-density parity-check code families, syndrome extraction circuits, and fault-tolerant architectures.
  • Low-Latency Decoding Algorithms: belief propagation and Best-First Ordered Statistics Decoding (BF-OSD) to explore error candidate spaces with drastically lower complexity.
  • Quantum Circuit Transpilation: transformer-based algorithms for mapping abstract quantum circuits to hardware-native gate sets and connectivity constraints (e.g., trapped ions and superconducting processors).

Best-First Ordered Statistics Decoding of Quantum LDPC Codes

Michele Banfi, Sebastiano Corli, Paolo Zentilini, Luca Barletta, Alberto Tarable, Marco Ferrari

Quantum Error Correction QLDPC Codes Ordered Statistics Decoding Belief Propagation
arXiv PDF
Abstract: We propose Best-First Ordered Statistics Decoding (BF-OSD), an algorithmic enhancement to the belief propagation plus ordered statistics decoding pipeline for quantum low-density parity-check (QLDPC) codes. By prioritizing error candidates through a best-first heuristic tree search over syndrome-compatible sub-spaces, BF-OSD achieves decoding performance on par with higher-order OSD while drastically cutting candidate exploration complexity and latency.

arXiv:2512.09834 [quant-ph] Dec 2025

Transpiling quantum circuits by a transformers-based algorithm

Michele Banfi, et al.

Quantum Transpilation Transformer Models Gate Synthesis Circuit Optimization
arXiv PDF
Abstract: Transpilation of quantum circuits to hardware-native topologies and gate libraries is a critical bottleneck in NISQ and early fault-tolerant quantum devices. In this work, we introduce an autoregressive transformer architecture that learns optimal gate substitution and routing strategies, mapping OpenQASM representations to target architectures (such as IonQ trapped-ion systems) with high fidelity and reduced gate counts.
